When people think about careers in quantitative finance, they often picture the
same handful of universities and financial centres. But a closer look at
Tübingen tells a more interesting story.

Graduates and researchers connected to the University of Tübingen have gone on
to work in financial stability, systematic investing, risk analysis and
financial technology. Their paths are different: some continued into doctoral
research, others moved into investment management, and some combined machine
learning with financial markets.

Here are a few examples.

## From Tübingen to financial stability at the ECB

**Katja Neugebauer** studied economics with a focus on quantitative methods at
the University of Tübingen and completed a doctorate in economics there.

She is a Senior Financial Stability Expert at the European Central Bank,
working in stress-test modelling. Her research interests include international
banking, bank risk and macroprudential policy. Before joining the ECB, she
worked at Banco de Portugal, from which she is temporarily on leave.
[LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/katja-neugebauer-07910274/) ·
[CEPR biography](https://cepr.org/about/people/katja-neugebauer)

Her path is a reminder that quantitative finance reaches beyond trading desks.
Central banks also need people who can analyse complex data, build models and
understand how risks move through the financial system.

## From a Tübingen master's to systematic investing

**Patrick Voßkamp** completed his master's in Economics and Finance at
Tübingen, focusing on econometrics and quantitative finance. His master's
thesis on factor investing received a research award and was written in
connection with an internship at Invesco.
[University of Tübingen](https://uni-tuebingen.de/uploads/media/201711_PreistraegerWiWi.pdf)

He went on to Allianz Global Investors, where he works in systematic equity
portfolio management. In systematic investing, investment decisions are
informed by models and rules that can be tested across data and market
conditions. [Professional profile](https://www.xing.com/profile/Patrick_VosskampCFA)

His story is particularly relevant for students wondering whether a Tübingen
master's can lead into quantitative investment work: it shows one route through
coursework, a research-focused thesis and industry experience.

## Mathematics, economics and quantitative portfolios

**Patrick Walker** studied mathematics and economics in Tübingen before
continuing his education in quantitative finance at ETH Zurich and the
University of Zurich. He is Head of Investment Solutions and Partner at OLZ,
where his areas of expertise include financial econometrics, portfolio
management and risk management.
[OLZ biography](https://olz.ch/en/about-us/team/patrick-walker)

His career illustrates how mathematical ideas connect to practical questions:
how should a portfolio be constructed, how can risk be measured, and what
happens when markets behave differently from a model's assumptions?

## From financial econometrics to insurance risk

**Dalia Elshiaty** completed doctoral research at the University of Tübingen and
describes her background as financial econometrics. Her professional profile
lists Allianz Lebensversicherungs-AG.
[Tübingen dissertation](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/141509/Elshiaty_Dissertation_Ver%C3%B6ffentlichteVersion.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) ·
[LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/dalia-elshiaty-3a7a95212/)

Her path highlights another area where quantitative skills matter:
understanding and managing financial risk in insurance. Statistical modelling
and financial data are useful far beyond the hedge funds that tend to dominate
conversations about quant careers.

## From machine learning research to investment firms

**Felix Leibfried** completed his doctoral research in artificial intelligence
in Tübingen. His career later took him into systematic investing: he worked as
a quantitative researcher at Eisler Capital and a quantitative developer at
iSAM Funds.

He is an AI Engineer at Millennium, working on AI for fixed income and
commodities. His story shows how skills developed in machine learning research
can transfer into financial applications, including roles that sit between
research and engineering.
[Personal website](https://www.felixleibfried.com/) ·
[German National Library dissertation record](https://d-nb.info/1144306558)

## Building a stronger community in Tübingen

These examples do not describe a single route into finance. They show the
opposite: Tübingen-connected researchers and graduates have taken different
combinations of mathematics, economics, statistics and computer science into a
wide range of roles.

That is also what we want to build on at the **Tübingen Quant Society**. We
want to bring together students who are curious about markets and
quantitative methods, create opportunities to learn from one another, and
connect the next generation of Tübingen students with people already working
in the field.

If you studied or conducted research in Tübingen and now work in quantitative
finance, financial technology or a related field, we would love to hear from
you. Whether you would be interested in giving a talk, sharing your experience
or simply connecting with students, get in touch with TQS.
