What can a degree in Tübingen lead to if you're interested in financial
markets? For Steffen Natter, it led to derivatives trading and eventually
to building a trading platform.

## Steffen Natter: From Tübingen to derivatives trading

Steffen Natter earned his Diplom-Kaufmann degree at the University of
Tübingen and went on to work in derivatives in Germany, London and Hong Kong.
He also co-founded vDEX, a digital derivatives trading platform.
[vDEX team biography](https://dev.vdex.ai/vDEX_Litepaper.pdf)

In a public post about a fund launch at Cumulus Capital, Natter described
strategies that used blockchain transaction data to look for price
discrepancies. It's one example of how quantitative traders can use a new
source of data to investigate a familiar market question.
[Steffen Natter's LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/steffen-natter_cumulus-capital-are-proud-to-announce-its-activity-6971359485445488640-p5St)

## Other paths into quantitative finance

The kind of problem you want to solve can help you decide where to start. In
systematic investing, you might test whether a signal holds up across market
conditions. In insurance, you might model how changing interest rates affect
long-term liabilities. At a central bank, you might use stress tests to
estimate how banks would handle a severe downturn.

The work draws on different combinations of mathematics, economics,
statistics and computer science. If you're choosing a thesis or side project,
start with a question you can test on real data. That gives you a concrete
way to explore which kind of work interests you.
